Change management is not bureaucratic red tape designed to slow down engineering. It is an operational governance framework that ensures changes are documented, rigorously tested, authorized, and auditable before they touch production.

At the center of this framework sits the change advisory board. The change advisory board is a cross-functional governing body made up of technical leads, infrastructure engineers, security personnel and business managers. Their mission is not to rubber stamp requests. It is to evaluate the operational risk, security implications and business disruption of any proposed modification to the enterprise environment. Every valid change begins with a formal approval process. If an engineer wants to upgrade an enterprise firewall or deploy a new software release, they cannot simply log into the production console and run the command. They must submit a formal change request. This request must clearly identify two critical human elements, ownership and stakeholders.

Ownership defines the specific engineer or team accountable for executing the change, monitoring the roll out and taking responsibility if something breaks. Stakeholders are the internal or external parties directly impacted by the change. If the database team plans to patch the customer billing system at

A maintenance window is an authorized time frame, typically during off- peak hours, such as 2:00 on a Sunday morning when system changes are permitted to minimize operational disruption. Finally, every action executed during that window must follow documented standard operating procedures. Standard operating procedures provide step-by-step instructions so that the execution is repeatable, consistent, and strictly adheres to enterprise baselines.
