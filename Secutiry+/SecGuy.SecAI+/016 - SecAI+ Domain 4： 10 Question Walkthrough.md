Welcome back to the lab. I am SEC guy. Builds and attacks are one thing, but staying out of court is another. SEC AI plus domain is about governance, compliance, and ethics. Our goal is to ensure that AI systems remain transparent, fair, and aligned with global standards like the NIST AIRMF and the EU AI act. Let's hit the questions. Question one, riskbased legislation. A company is deploying an AI system for real-time biometric identification in public spaces under the EU AI act. Which risk classification prohibits this specific use for most general applications? A minimal risk. B limited risk. C high risk. D unacceptable risk. The answer D. Unacceptable risk. The logic. The EU AI act identifies certain uses of AI that are too dangerous to fundamental rights.

Real time biometric surveillance is generally classified as unacceptable risk, meaning it is banned from the market with very few exceptions. Question two, interpretability mechanisms. A financial institution uses a model to approve loans, but needs a way to show which specific factors like debt to income ratio or credit history led to a rejection. Which concept ensures these AI decisions are understandable to humans? A model distillation, B. Explainable AI, XAI. C data normalization. D. Differential privacy. The answer. B. Explainable AI. X AI. The logic. X AI provides the tools and frameworks like SHAP or LIME needed to turn a blackbox prediction into a transparent explanation that a customer or regulator can understand.

Question three. Fairness implementation. A development team is scrubbing a data set to remove historical biases related to gender and age before the model training begins. At which stage of the mitigation process is this work occurring? A pre-processing B improcessing C post-processing D model pruning the answer A pre-processing. The logic when you fix the data before training it is pre-processing. If you adjust the algorithm during training it's inprocessing if you adjust the results after the model runs is post-processing. Question four standardized risk frameworks. Your organization needs a voluntary nonregulatory road map to manage AI risks. Which framework focuses specifically on the core functions of govern, map, measure, and manage? A ISO 27,01 B.

GDPRC NIST AI risk management framework D. PCIDSS. The answer C. NIST AI risk management framework. The logic. The NIST AIRMF is the industry standard for voluntary governance. It provides a structured way to integrate trustworthiness and risk management into every phase of the AI life cycle. Question five, operational oversight. A hospital uses AI to suggest surgical procedures, but the system requires a surgeon to manually confirm and sign off on every recommendation. Which ethical principle is being enforced? A full autonomy? B. Human in the loop? C. Model quantization. D. Static analysis. The answer. B. Human in the loop. The logic. Human in the loop ensures that in highstakes environments, a human remains the final authority, preventing the AI from making autonomous decisions without expert review.

Question six, data privacy regulations. Under the general data protection regulation, GDPR, a user asks an AI provider to remove all traces of their personal information, including the influence their data had on the model's training. Which right is being exercised? A right to portability. B. Right to rectification. C. Right to eraser. Right to be forgotten. D. Right to access. The answer. C. Right to eraser. Right to be forgotten. The logic. The right to eraser is a major compliance challenge for AI, often requiring machine unlearning to prove that a specific person's data no longer influences the model's weights. Question seven, transparency documentation. A developer provides a standardized document that details a model's training data, performance metrics, and intended use cases to help users determine if the model is safe for their project.

What is this document? A privacy policy, B. Model card, C system log, Data Manifesto. The answer B model card. The logic model cars act as the nutrition label for AI models. They provide the transparency needed for buyers and users to understand the model strengths and limitations. Question eight, impact assessment processes. A government agency plans to deploy a facial recognition system in a city center, but first conducts a formal study to evaluate how it might impact civil liberties and individual privacy. What is this study called? A penetration test. B vulnerability scan. C algorithmic impact assessment AIA. D static code review. The answer C. Algorithmic impact assessment AIA. The logic. An AIA is a proactive governance tool used to identify and mitigate potential negative societal impacts before an AI system is deployed.

Question nine, resource management. A company's leadership is concerned about the water and electricity consumed by the massive data centers used to train their LLM. Which pillar of responsible AI addresses these concerns? A. Social fairness. B. Environmental sustainability. Green AI. C. Data privacy. D. Ethical alignment. The answer. B. Environmental sustainability. Green AI. The logic. Green AI is the focus on making AI training and inference more energy efficient and reducing the overall carbon footprint of AI infrastructure. Question 10. Rights and ownership. A company is trying to determine who owns the legal rights to the code generated by an AI assistant during a developer's work shift. This issue primarily falls under which domain?

A model robustness? B. Intellectual property IP. C. Data integrity. D. Supply chain security. The answer B. Intellectual property IP. The logic intellectual property in AI involves the complex legal questions of who owns the outputs of generative systems. The human prompter, the model creator, or the employer.
