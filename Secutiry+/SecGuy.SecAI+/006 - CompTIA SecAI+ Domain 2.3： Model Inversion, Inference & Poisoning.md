All I need is an API key and a few thousand questions. By the time I'm done, I won't just have the answers. I will have a mathematical clone of their entire business. We are talking about model inversion, membership inference, model stealing, and the silent killer known as data poisoning.

This is the spy games edition. Let's start with model inversion. People assume that once an AI is trained, the original data is gone. They think the training data is like flour in a baked cake. You can't turn the cake back into flour. That is wrong. If a model overfits, meaning it memorized the training data too perfectly, an attacker can reverse the process. By repeatedly querying the model and analyzing the confidence scores, I can reconstruct the original input. Researchers have used this to reconstruct actual faces of people used to train facial recognition systems. I asked the AI, "What does John do look like?" It doesn't give me a picture, but the mathematical vectors allow me to rebuild his face pixel by pixel.

Close cousin to this is membership inference. The goal here isn't to see the data, but to know if the data was used. Imagine a cancer prediction model trained on patient records from a specific hospital. If I feed the model your medical record and it returns a confidence score of 99.9%, I know mathematically that your record was likely in the training set. Why does this matter? Because now I know you were a patient at that cancer hospital. This leads to attribute inference. Once I know you are in the data set, I can infer private details like your diagnosis, even if the model never explicitly states them. I am using the model's behavior to fill in the blanks of your private life.

Now, let's talk about model stealing or model extraction. This is intellectual property theft. Training a model like GPT4 costs hundreds of millions of dollars, but using it is cheap. An attacker can perform a distillation attack. I create a cheap empty student model. I send thousands of questions to your expensive teacher model. I take your answers and use them to train my student. Essentially, I am using your API to teach my AI. Another form of theft is adversarial reprogramming. This is where I take an AI designed for one task, like a medical image scanner, and trick it into performing a different task, like solving captures or mining cryptocurrency. I am stealing your compute resources for my own benefit.

Finally, the most insidious attack, data poisoning. This happens before the model is even trained. If I can hack into your data lake and change just 0.1% of the images, I can install a back door. This is called a split view attack. I teach the self-driving car. Stop at every stop sign unless the stop sign has a yellow sticky note on it. Then speed up. The model works perfectly in testing against 100% accuracy, but the moment the attacker puts a sticky note on a sign in the real world, the car crashes. This attacks the integrity of the training data and it is almost impossible to detect once the model is built.

So how do we stop this? We stop inversion with differential privacy adding noise to the data. We stop stealing by detecting high volume API queries.
