We have spent the last four videos breaking these models. We've injected them, poisoned them, and tricked them into revealing their secrets. Now, it is time to defend them. Welcome to domain 3. This is the blue team module. Today, we are building the shield. We are talking about guard rails, human in the loop, C2PA, and the new defense stack you need to pass the exam. If you are a security analyst, the exam wants you to know one thing. You cannot fix AI with a traditional firewall. Port 443 is always open. The traffic is encrypted and the attack looks like valid English. So, we need a new stack. It starts with input validation, often called sanitization.

Before a user's prompt ever touches your LLM, it must pass through a guardrail layer like Nvidia Nemo or Lang Chain validators. Think of this as a bouncer at the club. If the prompt contains phrases like ignore previous instructions or known injection patterns, the guard rail strips them out before the model even sees them. But what if the attack isn't an injection? What if it's a sponge attack designed to drain your wallet? The defense here is rate limiting. You must enforce strict limits on tokens per minute for every user. If an IP address tries to generate 50,000 words in 1 second, you cut them off before your cloud bill spikes. Now, let's talk about the output.

Output filtering is your safety net. We scan the AI's answer before sending it back. This is critical for stopping data leakage and detecting insecure code. If your developers use AI to write software, you must run those snippets through a SAS scanner to ensure the bot didn't write a back door into your app. Remember, AI writes code that works, but it doesn't always write code that is secure. This brings us to a critical new exam term, C2PA. The Coalition for Content Provenence and authenticity. How do we prove an image or text wasn't created by a deep fake bot? We use is safe 2PA cryptographically signed metadata. Think of it as a digital watermark that proves a human created the content.

Now let's look at the enterprise tools sie m and so a r in the AI era static rules don't work. Modern AI enhanced SIM uses UEBA user and entity behavior analytics. It learns the baseline. It knows that Bob from accounting usually queries the finance database at 9:00 a.m. If Bob suddenly asks the AI to export the entire customer list at 3:00 a.m., the SIM flags it as an anomaly. But, and this is huge for the exam, you cannot automate everything. For high-risk systems, you need human in the loop. If the AI wants to shut down a factory or deny a loan application, a human must review that decision. Finally, let's talk about privacy.

How do we train a model on sensitive medical data without reading the patients files? We use federated learning. Instead of sending the data to the cloud, we send the model to the phone. The phone trains itself locally and only sends the mathematical updates back. The raw data never leaves the device. We are just scratching the surface of AI defense.
