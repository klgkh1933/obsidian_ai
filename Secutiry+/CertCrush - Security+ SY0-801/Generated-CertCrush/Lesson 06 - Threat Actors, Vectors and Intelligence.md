## Threat actor classes and motivations · [↗ 01:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YHj9rKND0VY&t=75s)

- Nation-state/APT: espionage, persistence, strategic disruption; organized crime: profit and extortion.
- Hacktivists, competitors, insiders (malicious or accidental) and less capable attackers vary in resources and access.
- Evaluate capability, intent, access, target value and observed activity rather than treating the label as a risk score.

## Attack surface versus vector · [↗ 09:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YHj9rKND0VY&t=583s)

- [[Threat Modeling]]: **attack surface** is the set of exposed ways in; **attack vector** is a particular route or method.
- Examples: exposed admin API, compromised browser, spear-phishing email, malicious dependency, stolen access token.
- Third-party providers and AI model/data vendors extend the inherited attack surface.

## Threat intelligence lifecycle · [↗ 19:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YHj9rKND0VY&t=1192s)

- Collect, process, analyze and share evidence relevant to your systems and threat model.
- Prioritize quality and applicability of intelligence; record source, timeliness, confidence and limitations.
- Translate intelligence into justified control changes, monitoring and risk decisions.
