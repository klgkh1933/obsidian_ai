## 04:40 — The six control types ([↗](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iTlk_HxlCBg&t=280s))

the six control types. What job does it do? There are six. And this is where the exam spends most of its energy. Preventive controls stop a bad thing from happening in the first place. A locked door prevents entry. A firewall prevents unwanted traffic. Encryption prevents a thief from reading stolen data. Prevention is the front line. Detective controls spot a bad thing that's happening or already happened. They don't stop it, they notice it. A security camera detects an intruder. Corrective controls fix things after a bad event, getting you back to normal. Restoring from a backup after ransomware is corrective.

A fire extinguisher is corrective because it puts out the fire that already started. Corrective controls are about recovery and repair. ## 16:39 — Backing out vs failing forward ([↗](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iTlk_HxlCBg&t=999s))

thing. What do you do when the change goes sideways at 2 in the morning? Backing out means undoing the change and returning to the old state. Failing forward means not going back. You keep the new version and push a follow-up fix to repair whatever broke. Picture driving on a highway and missing your exit. You can't reverse down the shoulder. You fail forward. Carry on to the next exit and double back. On a quiet street, though, turning around is easy and safe. Backing out is usually the first choice when the old state is known. The roll back is tested and there's time left in the window. Failing forward wins when going back is risky or impossible.

Two common reasons. A database upgrade that already rewrote the data or a security patch where rolling back would reopen a floor attackers are actively using. Backup planning happens before the change. Failing forward is a decision you make during it. Next, the technical side.
